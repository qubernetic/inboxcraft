# 343

## Changelog
### Added
- `event.create` and `event.update` take an `alert`: `"none"` or the minutes before the start, at most a week, written
  as the event's alarm on the series and on each changed instance of it; an update moves only the first timed display or
  sound alarm and keeps every other alarm, a location alarm too, written as it came. `"none"` takes the timed display
  and sound alarms off, so the local alert no longer rings at the default ten minutes either. A new event without an
  `alert` is written with the default lead as its alarm (ten minutes before, a day before an all-day event), so other
  devices ring too. An update of the alert alone changes only the alarms: the times, `DTSTAMP`, `SEQUENCE` and the
  scheduling parameters stay and the guests are not told, and a changed instance that cannot be read is kept as it came
  while the others take the alert. `event.list` says each event's alert, `"custom"` where it has more than one display
  or sound alarm (#343)

## Gaps
- The event editor's Alert field is held by the form and saved through `event.create` and `event.update`, but the
  full editor and the window that draw it are not built yet; the small editor has no Alert field, as drawn (#343).
- A new event's Alert says the default lead it rings with (10 min before, 1 day before an all-day event) where the
  design's new-event frame says None (#343).
- The local alerts read an event's first 16 alarms, while `event.list` and the `alert` parameter look at all of them:
  an event with more than 16 alarms may show or move one that does not ring here (#343).
- How other calendar programs treat the `ACTION:NONE` alarm (RFC 9074) written for an alert of "none" is not checked
  yet; one that does not know it may ring for it or show an odd lead (#343).
- A full `event.update` (more than the alert) writes every alarm again and drops what is nested inside one, such as a
  location alarm's `VLOCATION`; an update of the alert alone keeps them (#343).
