# 260

## Changelog
### Changed
- Detect tries more ways, in this order: autoconfig at the domain (and the ISPDB when it is on), SRV records,
  `mail.`, `imap.` and `smtp.` of the domain itself, and last, as a guess about a third party, the provider domains
  of the domain's MX hosts (their autoconfig over HTTPS, then `mail.`, `imap.` and `smtp.` of them). A host whose
  probe timed out or whose name was not found is not tried again on another port. A host made up from a domain is tried only on the
  registered ports: IMAP 993 with TLS, then 143 with STARTTLS; submission 465 with TLS, then 587 with STARTTLS; never
  without encryption and never port 25 (#260)
- Detect probes its candidates: each is connected to, encrypted (TLS, or STARTTLS first), and must offer a sign-in
  the app can do; no user name or password is sent. It proposes the first IMAP and the first submission server that
  answered, and stops once it has both, after 24 probes or after 120 seconds. What it probed stands in the list of
  what it asked, and each probe that failed with its cause. A server the domain names (its autoconfig, the ISPDB or
  SRV) that no probe reached is still proposed, and the list says it was not checked; while the domain names servers
  of a kind, no common host name or MX provider's host is tried or proposed in their place. Common host names are no
  longer proposed unchecked: when the domain names nothing and nothing answers, nothing is proposed (#260)
### Fixed
- An address at a domain whose mail a provider hosts, where the domain answers nothing on the mail ports, is found
  through the provider named by the domain's MX records (`mail.<provider>`) (#260)

## Gaps
- Detect's provider domains come from the MX hosts without a public suffix list: one for each MX host, its last two
  labels, or three under a two-letter country label when the second last is three letters or fewer, at most two in
  all (`mx1.mail.example.net` gives `example.net`, `mx.example.co.uk` gives `example.co.uk`). So `mx.abc.de` gives
  `mx.abc.de` and `abc.de` is never tried, and a provider whose mail hosts are not under its MX hosts' domain is not
  found this way (#260).
- A host whose probe timed out is not probed again in the run, on any port: a host that drops only its port 993 and
  answers on 143 is missed (#260).
- On a network that blocks the mail ports, Detect proposes only what the domain names (unchecked) and finds nothing
  for a domain that names nothing; the account form and its check stay the way to enter it. A named server that
  answered and was wrong (no STARTTLS, a certificate fault, no sign-in) is not proposed, and no guess is tried in its
  place. The probe connects to names made up from the domain and the MX hosts, so the system looks those names up
  (#260).
- Detect's caps are checked before each lookup and probe; one that has started runs to its own timeouts (10 s to
  connect, 15 s for each read and write in the app), so a run can last somewhat past its 120 seconds. Cancelling the
  form drops the run's answer; the run itself goes on in the background until it ends or reaches its cap (#260).
- Detect proposes one IMAP and one submission server, the first that answered, not a list to choose from. The form
  has no words of its own yet for a server found through the MX hosts' provider: it shows it as a common server
  name, a guess to confirm; and an unchecked server is marked only by a line in the list of what failed (#260).
- Servers named by SRV records and autoconfig files keep the port they name (port 25 is left out); only the hosts
  Detect makes up from a domain are limited to 993, 143, 465 and 587 (#260).
